How engagements run

Embedded, hands-on, and validated against your real data before full build-out.

  1. Scope

    Requirements gathered directly from the people who use the thing, with technical trade-offs explained in plain business language rather than deferred to a document nobody reads.

  2. Prototype

    A rapid proof of concept built against your actual data and workflows, to establish feasibility before either of us commits to a full build.

  3. Build

    Iterating in real time on live feedback rather than waiting on handoff cycles. I’m in your office for this part, not on a status call.

  4. Prove it

    Outcomes quantified and presented to leadership — hours recovered, reconciliation breaks eliminated, decisions that used to take a week. Measured against the baseline the audit captured, so the comparison is real.

Questions

What owners ask before starting.

Is my company actually ready for AI, or is that overkill at our size?

Most mid-market companies are ready for a small piece of it, not an overhaul. Readiness comes down to two things: whether the data underneath is consistent enough to trust, and whether you have one repeatable, time-consuming process worth automating first. That is exactly what the audit measures, across eight dimensions, before anyone spends money on a build.

How much time does automation actually save?

It depends entirely on the process, and anyone who quotes you a number before seeing it is guessing. The figure that matters is how many hours a month someone senior currently spends rebuilding a report or re-keying data by hand. That gets measured during the audit and stated in hours, so the payback is arithmetic rather than a promise.

Will this increase revenue?

Sometimes — and I won’t quote you a percentage before I’ve seen your data. What the audit guarantees is narrower and more honest: a map of your systems, a readiness score, three opportunities priced against effort, and one working thing built on your real numbers. Most of the value in a first engagement is recovered time and decisions that stop being guesses. Revenue tends to follow that, but it follows it second.

Do you work with companies outside Houston?

Rarely, and not by preference. The work is built around being in the room, so engagements stay inside Houston, Sugar Land, Stafford, Missouri City, Katy, Pearland, The Woodlands, and the rest of Fort Bend and Harris County. If you’re further out and the problem is genuinely the right shape, ask — but the honest default is no.

How are engagements priced?

Fixed fee, always, at the figures in the table above. No hourly rates, because being fast should benefit both of us rather than costing me money. Nothing is quoted as a range once we’ve scoped it — the ranges above collapse to a single number in the proposal.

How do I know whether I need a one-off build or ongoing help?

If the audit surfaces one clearly defined thing worth fixing, that’s a single fix or an embedded build, and it ends when the thing works. If it surfaces a data function nobody owns, ongoing is the honest answer — a one-off build handed to a company with nobody to maintain it decays inside a year. The readout tells you which situation you’re actually in, and I’ll say so plainly even when the smaller answer is the right one.
